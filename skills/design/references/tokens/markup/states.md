# What does a state draw, and which selector shows it?

Read this when your own markup shows a state, such as hover, keyboard focus, selection, a pressed toggle, a checked checkbox or switch, a disabled control or an invalid field.

**Twenty roles answer the states of a control, and the second table says which attribute or pseudo-class shows each state.** `--fill-hover` covers a cell as well as a row. Focus, selection and invalidity reach utilities through `ring-edge-focus`, `outline-edge-focus`, `bg-selected/(--level-accent-soft-primary)`, `text-ink-selected`, `border-edge-selected`, `border-edge-invalid` and `text-ink-invalid`. The selected or current item of a navigation component takes `border-edge-tab-selected`, and any other selected item takes `border-edge-selected`. The focus width, the focus gap and the disabled fade have no key of their own. The three are read through arbitrary forms: `outline-[length:var(--bw-focus)]` or `ring-[length:var(--bw-focus)]`, `outline-offset-[var(--gap-focus)]` or `ring-offset-[var(--gap-focus)]`, and `opacity-[var(--fade-disabled)]`.

**A control of yours that is a checkbox or a switch reads what ArenaCheckbox and ArenaSwitch read.** The attribute table below names the roles a checked box or switch reads. The box takes `--fill-control` at rest and `--fill-control-on` when checked, and a checkbox draws `--edge-control` around it at rest and no edge when checked. A switch track draws no edge, so it reads the two fills alone. The knob takes `--fill-knob-off` and `--ink-knob-off` when off, and `--fill-knob` and `--ink-knob` when on. The tick of a checkbox takes `--emphasis-primary-ink`. A colour slot answer moves Arena's components, and markup of yours reads roles, so a slot does not reach your control.

## A toggle that shows every state

`arena-interactive` paints the background and ink of a control of yours in every state, as Arena's navigation rows do. Your own fill beats every fill the class paints, the hover and the selected wash included. An element with a fill of its own writes those states from the roles in the tables above, and keeps the class for its ink. At rest the fill is transparent and the ink is `--ink-muted` at `--level-ink-muted`. Under the pointer the fill takes `--fill-hover` and the ink `--ink-body` at `--level-ink-body`. A press mixes the fill toward the ink by `--mix-pressed`. While `aria-pressed`, `aria-selected` or `aria-current` is true, the fill washes with `--fill-selected` at `--level-accent-soft-primary` and the ink reads `--ink-selected`. Under the pointer or a press, the wash mixes toward `--ink-selected` by `--mix-hover` or `--mix-pressed`. A disabled control keeps `--fade-disabled` of its opacity, and the pointer and a press leave it as it is. `arena-focus` draws `--edge-focus` at `--bw-focus`, `--gap-focus` away from the edge, while the control holds keyboard focus. `arena-invalid` draws `--edge-invalid` on a field's edge while `aria-invalid` is true, and `arena-invalid__message` draws its message in `--ink-invalid`. The classes read roles and their levels, so a colour slot answer does not reach them. Both packages ship them in `css/vocabulary/state.css`, which a page without Tailwind takes too.

```html
<button type="button" aria-pressed="false" class="arena-interactive arena-focus">
  Notify me
</button>

<label for="email">Email address</label>
<input id="email" aria-invalid="true" aria-describedby="email-error" class="arena-invalid arena-focus border-edge-field border-[length:var(--bw-field)]" />
<p id="email-error" class="arena-invalid__message">Enter an address such as name@example.com.</p>
```

A rule of yours, a utility of yours and your plugin sheet beat a markup class, because the classes sit in the `components` layer. To override one, write your rule. To draw a state another way, write the roles of the tables above on an element that wears none of the classes.

A nested style plugin's answer reaches `--edge-focus` and the other roles, and your markup reads those roles, never `--focus-ring`. Under a nested plugin class `--edge-focus` follows the subtree's answer, while `--focus-ring` keeps the root's.

## A checkbox and a switch of your own

A checkbox of yours hides the native box with `appearance: none` and fills it with `--fill-control`. The tick is a rotated corner in `--emphasis-primary-ink`, stroked at `--bw-strong` as ArenaCheckbox strokes its own. A switch of yours is a track in the two control fills and a knob in the two knob fills. The example passes `arena audit` in either layer.

```html
<label class="own-check">
  <input type="checkbox" class="own-check__box arena-focus" />
  Email me a weekly summary
</label>

<span id="dark-label">Dark mode</span>
<button type="button" role="switch" aria-checked="false" aria-labelledby="dark-label" class="own-switch arena-focus">
  <span class="own-switch__knob"></span>
</button>
```

```css
.own-check__box {
  appearance: none;
  inline-size: var(--sp-5);
  block-size: var(--sp-5);
  border: var(--bw-control) solid var(--edge-control);
  border-radius: var(--r-control);
  background: var(--fill-control);
}
.own-check__box:checked {
  border-color: transparent;
  background: var(--fill-control-on);
}
.own-check__box:checked::after {
  content: "";
  display: block;
  inline-size: var(--sp-1);
  block-size: var(--sp-2);
  margin: var(--sp-1) auto 0;
  border: solid var(--emphasis-primary-ink);
  border-width: 0 var(--bw-strong) var(--bw-strong) 0;
  rotate: 45deg;
}
.own-switch {
  display: flex;
  border: none;
  inline-size: var(--sp-10);
  padding: var(--sp-0);
  border-radius: var(--r-pill);
  background: var(--fill-control);
}
.own-switch[aria-checked="true"] {
  justify-content: flex-end;
  background: var(--fill-control-on);
}
.own-switch__knob {
  inline-size: var(--sp-5);
  block-size: var(--sp-5);
  border-radius: var(--r-pill);
  background: var(--fill-knob-off);
}
.own-switch[aria-checked="true"] .own-switch__knob {
  background: var(--fill-knob);
}
```
