# Where does a layer of mine sit among the layers Arena draws?

Read this when a layer of yours, such as a sticky bar, a panel or a popover, must sit above or below the layers Arena draws.

A layer of yours reads `--z-*`, the same scale Arena's layers read. The table below lists the layers in stacking order. A value shared by two layers resolves by DOM order rather than by design, so take a step and never a number of your own.

**Arena's anchored panels stack on their own rules, and a `z-index` of yours does not move them.** In React, ArenaMenu, ArenaPopover, ArenaTooltip, ArenaCombobox and ArenaMultiSelect open their panel in the top layer (`popover="manual"`), which stacks above every step on the table, an ArenaDialog and the toast included, in the order the panels were shown. No `z-index` of yours reaches above it. In Angular, those five components place their panel in the CDK overlay pane, which sits just under `--z-toast` and above every other step.

| custom property | what sits there |
| --- | --- |
| `--z-nav` | Fixed page navigation: a bottom bar, a sticky top bar. Arena draws one of these, ArenaBottomNav, and the step is not there for it: it is there because the invariant is system-wide and a consumer's own shell is part of the system, so a host's sticky header and Arena's bar interleave with Arena's overlays by design rather than by DOM order. The step is the system's, whether or not a component stands on it. Below dropdown, so an ArenaMenu opened from the bar covers it rather than resolving by DOM order, which is the failure this family exists to end. Anything drawn against this step also reads --pad-safe-bottom, the device's bottom inset, and --layout-bar for its own height. |
| `--z-skip` | The skip link, while it holds focus. It is the one thing on the page that must cover the furniture it exists to bypass: a link that lands under a sticky header is a link nobody can read, and equal numbers resolve by DOM order, which puts the header on top because the skip link is deliberately first in the document. So it is above nav and no higher: everything from sheet upward is a surface that has taken the interaction, and a skip link floating over a modal is a route out of a trap that is meant to hold. |
| `--z-sheet` | A non-modal panel anchored to an edge: a cart, a filter drawer, a detail pane that leaves the page usable behind it. Arena draws one of these, ArenaSheet, and the step exists for the same reason nav does: a host's own edge panel stands beside it on the same step. Above nav, because a sheet that slides over a fixed bar and lands under it is the failure; below dropdown, because an ArenaMenu opened from inside the sheet belongs over it. It is NOT modal: a panel that takes the whole interaction is an ArenaDialog, which sits above dropdown and tooltip and has a scrim to say so. |
| `--z-dropdown` | A panel anchored to its trigger: ArenaMenu's and ArenaPopover's, and a calendar event's on the same step. The step orders such a panel only where it is in the page; a panel shown in the top layer stacks by the order it was shown, above every step here, a dialog and the toast stack included, and a panel in an overlay pane stacks just under the toast step. |
| `--z-tooltip` | above dropdown, so a tooltip on a menu item wins |
| `--z-modal` | ArenaDialog |
| `--z-modal-nested` | ArenaConfirmDialog, which opens from an ArenaDialog |
| `--z-palette` | ArenaCommandPalette |
| `--z-onboarding` | the coachmark and the scrim beneath it: one step, two uses. The scrim takes the step and the coachmark is drawn inside it, so the two are separated by containment rather than by a second number; nothing else on the page may sit between them, which is what a single step means here. |
| `--z-toast` | floats above everything, including onboarding: a transient notice raised by an action taken inside a dialog must stay visible |
